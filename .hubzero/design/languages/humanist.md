# Humanist

Humanist design communicates that a person is on the other side of the interface.

It is warm without being casual, approachable without being childlike, and calm without being austere. Where Minimal removes to create quiet and Organic softens to create comfort, Humanist adjusts *proportion and warmth* to create the sense of being addressed by someone rather than processed by something.

The name is typographic in origin, and the origin is the language. Humanist letterforms carry the trace of a hand — modulated strokes, open apertures, calligraphic proportion — and that quality of considered warmth extends to every other decision.

This is a language for institutions that need to feel trustworthy *and* human at once: healthcare, education, public service, nonprofits. The failure it prevents is the one those organizations most often make — a site so concerned with appearing credible that it stops sounding like people.

---

# Decision Hierarchy

When two subsystems conflict, resolve in this order:

1. **Typographic warmth** — the type carries the humanity.
2. **Reading comfort** — measure, rhythm, and clarity at length.
3. **Spacing** — generous, human-scaled.
4. **Colour** — warm neutrals with considered, unsaturated accents.
5. **Photography** — real people, real moments.
6. **Corner and border** — gently softened, quiet.
7. **Motion** — calm and supportive.

Humanist resolves toward the option a person would find easier and more welcoming — but never at the cost of clarity. Warmth that obscures information has failed, because the audiences this language serves are usually trying to accomplish something that matters to them.

**Against Organic:** Organic evokes *nature* — soft forms, natural materials, earthy palettes, unhurried pacing. Humanist evokes *people* — humanist letterforms, human scale, considered proportion. Organic's radius is generous and its palette earthy; Humanist's radius is gentle and its palette warm-neutral. Organic wants a visitor to feel calm; Humanist wants them to feel addressed.

**Against Minimal:** Minimal reduces until only the essential remains, and its neutrality is deliberate. Humanist keeps warmth that Minimal would remove as unnecessary — a slightly warm ground, a modulated typeface, a photograph of a person. A Humanist page stripped of its warmth is a Minimal page.

**Against Classic:** Classic communicates institutional permanence through proportion and symmetry. Humanist communicates approachability through the same care applied differently — asymmetric where useful, warm rather than formal, contemporary rather than established.

---

# Signature Traits

- Humanist letterforms with visible calligraphic origin
- Warm neutrals rather than cool greys
- Human scale — nothing oversized, nothing cramped
- Generous reading measure and comfortable rhythm
- Photography of real people in real situations
- Gently softened geometry, never sharp and never pillowy
- Plain, direct language supported by a plain, direct system

---

# Typography Language

Typography is the language's foundation and its namesake.

The type strategy is a **humanist sans or humanist serif** — a face whose letterforms carry stroke modulation, open apertures, and calligraphic proportion. These are the qualities that make text feel written rather than generated, and they are legible at small sizes precisely because open apertures aid character recognition.

This is a strategy that explicitly excludes the neutral geometric grotesque. A face constructed from circles and straight lines has no hand in it, and the absence is exactly what this language is built to avoid. A page set in a geometric sans is not Humanist regardless of its palette.

Hierarchy is established through a moderate scale with clear steps, supported by weight. Nothing is set at a size that shouts — the display end is confident rather than dramatic, because human scale means nothing addresses the visitor from above.

Reading comfort is a primary requirement. Generous line height, a measure that supports sustained reading, and paragraph spacing that gives text room. These audiences frequently read carefully because the content matters to them.

Where a second family is used, it should share the humanist quality — pairing a humanist sans with a humanist serif is natural; pairing either with a geometric or technical face is not.

---

# Colour Language

The palette is warm neutral.

Grounds carry a trace of warmth — a paper white, a soft cream, a warm off-white — rather than pure white or cool grey. Ink is a warm near-black or deep brown-grey rather than pure black. These two choices establish more of the language's character than any accent will.

Accents are considered and unsaturated: muted blues, soft greens, warm terracottas, dusty golds. Colours a thoughtful person would choose, not colours engineered for conversion. Saturation should stay below what Modern or Playful would use.

Colour carries meaning where meaning is needed — a status, a category, a call to action — and otherwise stays out of the way. In a language this quiet, an accent goes a long way.

**Warm and desaturated palettes sit closest to the accessibility threshold**, and they are the palettes most likely to look obviously legible while failing measurement. Verify every foreground value against every warm surface it appears on, not against white. See `.hubzero/design/principles.md` — The Contrast Contract.

---

# Motion Language

Motion is calm, brief, and supportive.

Transitions ease gently and settle without bounce. Nothing snaps, nothing overshoots, nothing draws attention to itself. Motion confirms that something happened and helps a visitor keep their place — it does not perform.

Duration sits in a comfortable middle: slower than a technical interface, faster than a luxury one. The feeling should be of an interface responding attentively rather than either reacting instantly or making the visitor wait.

Restraint in quantity matters more than character in any single transition. A small number of considered transitions produces more coherence than animating every section.

---

# Photography Language

Photography shows real people in real situations, and it is where the language most obviously succeeds or fails.

Subjects are genuine and unposed — a clinician talking with a patient, a teacher with students, a person at work. Natural light, ordinary environments, and expressions that read as unperformed. The visitor should believe they are seeing a moment rather than a shoot.

Avoid corporate stock photography entirely. Staged groups around a laptop, arms-crossed confidence poses, and impossibly diverse meeting rooms all read as false, and falseness is fatal to a language built on trustworthiness.

Warmth in the grade should be consistent with the palette — slightly warm, naturally lit, never heavily filtered or dramatically graded.

Where real photography of people cannot be produced honestly — and in a blueprint depicting a fictional organization, it frequently cannot — prefer environmental photography, considered abstraction, or authored illustration over generated portraits presenting as real individuals. See `.hubzero/experience/content.md` — Fictional Content Policy and `.hubzero/experience/photography.md`.

---

# Corner Language

Corners carry a gentle, moderate radius — enough to remove hardness, never enough to read as soft or playful.

This is a deliberate middle position and the value matters. Too sharp reads as technical or institutional; too round reads as Playful or Organic. Humanist sits between them, and the radius should look considered rather than default.

Apply it consistently across every component. The warmth here comes from the type and the palette; corners simply avoid contradicting them.

---

# Border Language

Borders are light and used for genuine structure.

A hairline separating sections, defining a field, or grouping related content. Weight stays consistent and quiet. Borders never decorate.

Border colour should be drawn from the warm palette rather than a neutral grey — a cool border on a warm ground is a small inconsistency that accumulates across a page into something a visitor notices without identifying.

---

# Shadow Language

Shadow is soft, subtle, and sparing.

Where an element genuinely needs to lift — a menu, a dialog — a gentle, diffuse shadow with a warm tint rather than a neutral one. Most surfaces need no elevation at all; separation is achieved through space and warm tonal shifts.

Heavy or tightly-focused shadows read as mechanical and contradict the language.

---

# Spacing Language

Spacing is generous and human-scaled.

Content has room to breathe without the expansive emptiness of Luxury or the strict mathematics of Swiss. The rhythm should feel comfortable — the pacing of a well-made book or a considered printed document rather than a system-generated layout.

Reading passages get particular attention: sufficient margin around text, comfortable paragraph spacing, and a measure that does not require effort.

Density is acceptable where a visitor is scanning for something specific — a directory, a schedule, a list of services — provided the reading passages elsewhere retain their space. Humanist serves institutions with a lot to communicate, and refusing all density would make those sites harder to use.

---

# Interaction Language

Interactions are gentle, clear, and unambiguous.

Hover states shift tone or reveal a soft underline. Active states are clear without being forceful. Everything communicates plainly what will happen.

Focus indicators must be genuinely visible. A soft warm palette makes a subtle focus ring easy to miss, and this language serves audiences who disproportionately rely on keyboard navigation — public services, healthcare, and education all have accessibility obligations that go beyond a preference.

Interactive elements should be obviously interactive. In a warm, quiet system there is no visual noise to hide affordances behind, so ambiguity is a design failure rather than a discoverability challenge.

Prefer native elements. They are more accessible, more familiar, and more consistent with a language built on meeting people where they are — see `.hubzero/principles.md` — Native Before Custom.

---

# Component Language

Components are plain, warm, and unfussy.

A button is a clearly labelled shape with a gentle radius and an obvious state. A card is a warm surface with comfortable padding. A form field is generously sized with a visible label — never a placeholder standing in for one.

Forms deserve particular care, because this language's architectures are form-heavy: appointment requests, applications, enrolments, enquiries. Visible labels, clear help text, plainly stated requirements, and error messages written as a person would say them.

Nothing should feel like a control panel. Components should feel like parts of a considered document that happen to be interactive.

---

# Visual Rhythm

A Humanist page reads as something a careful person made for other people.

Rhythm comes from the alternation of comfortable reading passages, warm surfaces, and honest photography — with enough variation to sustain a long page and enough consistency that nothing surprises.

Asymmetry is permitted and often useful — an indented passage, an offset image, a sidebar of related information. It should feel like considered layout rather than compositional ambition.

The overall impression is of unhurried competence. Nothing shouts, nothing hides, and a visitor never wonders what a thing is for.

---

# Responsive Behavior

Humanist translates to mobile well, and its risk is quiet erosion rather than dramatic failure.

Reading measure is the primary verification. These architectures carry substantial explanatory content, and a measure tuned for desktop reading can become uncomfortable on a phone. Check it explicitly at handheld width.

Warmth must survive. A common mobile regression is losing warm surface tones to a plain white background for perceived simplicity — which removes the language while looking perfectly acceptable.

Spacing loosens rather than tightens where reading is involved. Compressing text passages to reduce scrolling works directly against the comfort this language depends on.

Touch targets should be generous — comfortably beyond the accessibility minimum. Human scale means not requiring precision.

Forms need real mobile attention, since this is where most conversions happen for these architectures: single-column layout, correctly typed inputs, visible labels that stay visible, and errors that appear beside the field they concern.

Photography of people needs mobile crops that keep the subject's face and context. A wide environmental shot scaled down loses exactly the human element it was chosen for.

Navigation stays plain and clearly labelled. Institutions serving broad audiences should not require pattern literacy to find things.

# Token Contract

Humanist must own the following rather than inherit them.

* **Type family** — a humanist sans or serif with genuine stroke modulation and open apertures. This is the language's defining decision, and an inherited geometric or neutral system font negates it entirely.
* **Colour** — a warm neutral ground and ink, plus unsaturated accents. An inherited cool grey scale is the single most common way this language is lost, because the result still looks clean and professional.
* **Border colour** — drawn from the warm palette rather than a neutral grey.
* **Radius** — a gentle, moderate value chosen deliberately between technical sharpness and playful softness.
* **Spacing scale** — generous, with comfortable steps and additional room reserved for reading passages.
* **Line height** — tuned for sustained reading rather than for interface text.
* **Motion duration and easing** — calm and settling, with no overshoot.
* **Shadow** — soft, diffuse, and warm-tinted where present.

Warm palettes make contrast failures likelier and less visible. Verify every foreground against every warm surface and record the ratios, so a later palette adjustment cannot silently undo the fix.

# Suitable Architectures

- Healthcare
- Education
- Nonprofit
- Government
- Services
- Community
- Corporate

Humanist suits institutions that need to be trusted by a broad public and whose audiences include people under stress, in unfamiliar circumstances, or with a wide range of abilities and technical confidence.

It is a poor fit where a brand needs to feel exclusive, cutting-edge, or dramatic — Luxury retail, Automotive, and consumer Marketing campaigns all want a register this language deliberately avoids. It is also a poor fit for dense technical reference material, where Technical or Swiss will serve the reader better.

# Anti-Patterns

**Never:**

- Use a neutral geometric grotesque. A face with no hand in it is the exact absence this language exists to correct.
- Use cool greys. Warm neutrals are the palette, and a cool scale removes the language while still looking professional.
- Ship staged corporate stock photography. Falseness is fatal to a language built on trustworthiness.
- Present generated portraits as real named individuals.
- Use placeholder text in place of a visible form label.
- Write error messages in system language rather than plain human language.
- Use a subtle focus indicator on a soft warm palette.
- Compress reading passages on mobile to reduce scrolling.
- Replace warm surfaces with plain white on mobile.
- Oversize display type. Human scale means nothing addresses the visitor from above.
- Use saturated accent colours at Modern or Playful intensity.

**Specifically not Organic:** Organic evokes nature through generous rounding, earthy palettes, and unhurried pacing. Humanist evokes people through letterforms, proportion, and warm neutrals. If the radius is generous and the palette earthy, it is Organic.

**Specifically not Minimal:** Minimal removes warmth as unnecessary. If the ground is pure white, the ink pure black, and the type neutral, the language has become Minimal.

**Specifically not Playful:** warmth is not friendliness performed. Bright colour, bouncy motion, and generous rounding belong to Playful; Humanist is warm and calm rather than warm and energetic.
